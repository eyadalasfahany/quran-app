Overview

A calm, Arabic-first system for reading, listening to and memorizing the Quran. The mushaf is the hero; everything else steps back.

Content fundamentals

  • Write the interface in clear Modern Standard Arabic, right to left. Keep sentences short and warm: "تابع القراءة", "وِردك اليوم: ٤ صفحات".
  • Use Arabic-Indic digits (١٢٣) in Arabic copy, for ayah, page and juz numbers alike.
  • Encourage, never guilt. Progress copy says "متقدّم بيوم" ("a day ahead"), never "you broke your streak". No streak counters, no exclamation marks, no emoji.
  • Address the reader directly and respectfully. No marketing language around Quran content.
  • Quran text is never edited. Render it exactly as supplied by the content source, in its own font. Never typeset it from a keyboard, never let the interface font render it, and never crop, truncate or ellipsize an ayah.

Color

  • Appearance follows the system by default. The default, Automatic («تلقائي»), uses light or dark to match the device. In reader settings people can choose light, sepia or dark for reading instead; the app can switch to dark after Maghrib if they opt in.
  • Screens sit on surface; the Quran itself sits on surface-page; cards and rows use surface-raised with a line hairline.
  • Body text is ink, secondary text ink-muted. Both pass 4.5:1 on every surface in every theme.
  • brand (deep green) is for the one primary action on a screen, the active tab and progress. The continue-reading card uses brand-surface, which stays deep green in every theme, with on-brand-surface text. Text on a brand fill is on-brand, never literal white.
  • gold is ornament only: the page frame, surah cartouche (pointed double frame with an eight-pointed star ۞ in each end), ayah end marks (﴿ ﴾), the ﴿ ﴾ around the Basmala, and the Continue reading rosette (gold-on-brand-surface #c9a961 at 35%). The cartouche and rosette outlines draw in (700 ms) the first time they scroll into view, and appear drawn with Reduce Motion on. Gold text uses gold-ink.
  • The audio-synced ayah sits on highlight-active, a near-neutral wash (never gold). The recited state is carried by its end-of-ayah mark (﴿ ﴾), which turns brand, and in Reading mode by a 3px brand rule on its leading edge, so it reads apart from every user highlight in every theme. In page-exact Mushaf (QCF), where the marker is a font glyph, the marker is never recoloured: a 3px brand rule in the page margin beside each line of the recited ayah carries the state instead.
  • User highlights use highlight-gold, highlight-green, highlight-blue and highlight-rose. In light and sepia they step down in lightness in that order, so they stay apart without hue. In dark they can't (ink needs 7:1), so colour is never the only cue: the picker shows the chosen swatch with a check and every swatch has its name, lists show the colour name, and each highlighted ayah's accessibility label ends with its colour name.
  • One meaning per colour. Gold fills mean a gold user highlight and nothing else. Search matches inside Quran text get a brand outline (qa-match), never a fill or a weight change. "Last read" uses surface-raised with a brand bookmark glyph. Today in the khatma plan is an outlined brand cell.
  • The full-screen player is always dark: player-bg, player-ink, player-muted, player-accent.
  • Keyboard and switch-control focus is a solid 2px focus-ring outline at a 2px offset.
  • Supporting colours: fill-secondary for segment tracks, input fills, progress tracks, monogram tiles and disabled buttons; line-strong for control borders (inputs, code boxes, unselected chips, page dots); on-brand-surface-muted for secondary text on brand-surface; player-line for rims and dividers on player-bg. Charts use brand, gold, chart-blue and chart-gray (each 3:1 or better on surface-raised), always with a text legend that carries the values. No colour outside these tokens.
  • Increase Contrast. When the system setting is on (iOS isDarkerSystemColorsEnabled, Android isHighTextContrastEnabled), each theme swaps in its high-contrast line, line-strong, ink-muted and glass-stroke: hairlines reach 3:1 or better and control borders 4:1 or better, on surface, surface-raised and fill-secondary. The theme stays the app's own choice; the override is per theme. Try it with the Increase Contrast button above.

Typography

  • Quran text uses the quran family. The user chooses the script in settings (Uthmani Hafs, Madani page-exact, IndoPak via quran-indopak, Uthmani Tajweed). Production fonts are the King Fahd Complex and Quran Foundation font files bundled in the app; Amiri Quran is only the design-time fallback.
  • Sizes: quran-mushaf in Mushaf mode, quran-reading in Reading mode, quran-focus in Focus mode and the player, quran-word in word chips. Let readers scale Quran text from 80% to 160% without scaling the interface.
  • Surah names and greetings use display-sm, display-md and display-lg (Amiri). Ayah and page numbers use numeral.
  • Everything else uses the ui family (IBM Plex Sans Arabic): title 22, headline 17, body 17, subhead 15, caption 13, label 12. No other interface sizes, and nothing below 12.
  • Dynamic Type. Each ui style scales with its iOS text style (RN dynamicTypeRamp): title→title2, headline→headline, body→body, subhead→subheadline, caption→footnote, label→caption1. Content text (body, translations, tafsir, row titles) is uncapped; tab and sidebar labels and segment labels cap at maxFontSizeMultiplier 1.3 and use the large-content viewer instead. At accessibility sizes, rows stack their trailing value under the title rather than truncating. Quran text keeps its own 80–160% reader scale.
  • Bold Text. When the system Bold Text setting is on (iOS), every ui style moves up one weight (400→500, 500→600, 600→700, 700 stays). The UI font is custom, so the app does this itself. Quran fonts never change weight.

Display modes and scrolling

  • Three display modes, chosen in a SegmentedControl: Mushaf (page-exact lines), Reading (ayah list with translation) and Focus (one ayah, large).
  • Every mode scrolls vertically. Pages stack top to bottom with a thin page and juz divider between them; there is no horizontal page swiping. The list is virtualized, and jumping to an ayah lands it at the top.
  • The reading header and the tab bar minimize while scrolling down and return on scroll up, so the text has the whole screen.
  • Every gesture has a visible alternative. Ayah actions open on long-press, on a tap on the ayah's marker (its hit area grows to 44pt), and from the ayah-actions GlassButton, which acts on the recited or top-visible ayah. A tap on a word stays the WordChip meaning. Focus mode has previous and next ayah buttons as well as the swipe. Every ayah also exposes its actions to VoiceOver and TalkBack as accessibility actions.

Liquid Glass

  • Glass lives only in the navigation and control layer that floats above content: the tab bar, the mini-player, floating icon buttons, segmented controls and sheets. Never put glass on Quran text, cards or list rows.
  • Use at most one glass layer at a time. Never stack glass on glass. Group neighbouring controls into one glass container rather than several separate capsules: at most one group per screen edge, plus back. A title over content is plain text or sits inside that group, never its own capsule.
  • Content fades under floating controls with a scroll edge effect: native on iOS 26, a surface-page-to-transparent gradient elsewhere. Quran text is never legible directly behind a control.
  • On iOS 26 and later, render glass with the native material: GlassView from expo-glass-effect (glassEffectStyle="regular" for bars and sheets; "clear" only over the player artwork). Wrap groups in GlassContainer.
  • Check isLiquidGlassAvailable() and AccessibilityInfo.isReduceTransparencyEnabled(). When glass is unavailable or Reduce Transparency is on, fall back to glass-fallback (solid) with a line hairline. Android and iOS below 26 always get the fallback.
  • Never fade a glass view by lowering its opacity; animate its style between regular and none instead. With Reduce Motion on, switch the style without animating it: animating into or out of a blur is itself motion.
  • In designs and on the web, approximate glass with glass-fill, a blur-glass backdrop blur, a 1px glass-stroke rim and shadow-glass; the selected item inside glass sits on glass-selected. The .qa-glass class in bundle.css does this, including the Reduce Transparency fallback.

Spacing, radii and layout

  • Screens use a space-4 side gutter; floating glass sits space-4 from the screen edges. Cards pad space-5; home sections are space-5 apart.
  • Radii nest: radius-pill for buttons, the tab bar and segmented controls; radius-xl for sheets and player artwork; radius-lg for cards and the mini-player; radius-md for tiles and chips; radius-sm for highlights.
  • Touch targets are at least 44 × 44pt (48 × 48dp on Android). A control drawn smaller (chips, segments, ayah markers, small icon buttons) extends its hit area to that size; neighbouring targets don't overlap.
  • Mirror layout for right-to-left, including audio. Progress follows the reading direction: the player bar, mini-player line and download bars fill from the right, with elapsed time on the right. Previous and next follow the reading order, so next ayah sits on the left and its glyph points left. The play and pause glyphs never flip. Numbers keep their digit order.

Motion

  • Motion is soft and short: 200–300ms ease-out for sheets and tab changes, a 150ms cross-fade for the ayah highlight. Durations with ease-out are for opacity and color: duration-instant (80ms) for the word highlight, which has to keep up with the recitation; duration-fast (150ms) for the ayah highlight cross-fade; duration-standard (200ms) for tab and small state changes; duration-slow (300ms) for Reduce Motion fades. Anything that moves or resizes uses the spring (spring-duration 350ms, spring-damping-ratio 1, so it settles without bouncing) and follows the finger while it is dragged. Sheets, the full player and the tab bar use the native components, which bring the system motion. The audio highlight moves smoothly from ayah to ayah, never flashing.
  • Respect Reduce Motion: replace slides with fades, and turn off auto-scroll animation (jump instead).
  • Let people stop motion. Any touch on the reader stops auto-scroll at once and shows a pill to return to the current ayah; following resumes only when it is tapped.

Iconography

  • Line icons on a 24px grid with a 1.75px stroke, round caps and round joins, in ink or brand. On iOS, prefer the matching SF Symbol where one exists.
  • Every icon-only button has an Arabic accessibility label ("بحث", "تشغيل").
  • One glyph per destination, everywhere (tab bar, sidebar, Home tiles): Home house, Mushaf book, Listen headphones, My Quran and Saved bookmark, Hadith text.quote, Surahs list.bullet, Khatma checkmark.circle, Downloads arrow.down.circle, Settings gearshape. Never use one glyph for two places, and never pencil for anything but editing.
  • No decorative icons beside Quran text. The only ornaments around the Quran are the gold frame, cartouche, ayah markers and the ﴿ ﴾ around the Basmala.

Components

  • Button, GlassButton, TabBar, MiniPlayer, BottomSheet and SegmentedControl are the control layer.
  • Ayah, SurahHeader, WordChip and HighlightSwatch render Quran content and its annotations.
  • ScriptPicker lets the reader choose the Quran font; ReciterRow lists reciters.
  • Toggles are the platform Switch (system track colours, on-tint brand), never a drawn one. On iOS 26 segmented controls and sheets are the system ones too.
  • The previews are HTML with the classes in components/bundle.css (prefix qa-). The React Native app implements the same tokens and anatomy natively.

Ayah

Mushaf mode with the recited ayah and a user highlight; Reading mode

One ayah of Quran text followed by its marker, in Mushaf, Reading or Focus mode, with optional recitation and user highlights.

  • Provide: the verse key, the ayah text exactly as supplied by the content source, the chosen script and mode, the highlight colour if any, and whether it is being recited.
  • Classes: a container qa-quran (plus qa-quran-reading or qa-quran-focus for those modes), each ayah in qa-ayah, followed by qa-marker with the number in numeral.
  • Recited ayah: qa-ayah-active (highlight-active, a near-neutral wash) and qa-marker-active (the marker fills with brand, numeral in on-brand) where the app draws the marker. In Reading mode a 3px brand rule marks the leading edge. In page-exact Mushaf (QCF) the marker is a font glyph and is never recoloured; a 3px brand rule in the page margin beside each line of the recited ayah takes its place. It cross-fades in 150ms as the audio moves on. None of this touches the Quran glyphs.
  • User highlights: qa-hl-gold, qa-hl-green, qa-hl-blue, qa-hl-rose. A recited ayah keeps its user highlight fill and adds the filled marker and, in Reading mode, the leading rule. The ayah's accessibility label ends with the highlight's colour name («مظلّلة بالأخضر»).
  • Search matches inside an ayah: qa-match, a brand outline around the matched words. Never a fill, a colour or a weight change on the glyphs.
  • Reading mode places the translation under the ayah in qa-translation, in the translation's own language and direction.
  • In Mushaf mode, line breaks come from the mushaf layout data. Never let text wrap decide where an ayah breaks.
  • Ayah actions BottomSheet opens three ways: long-press on the ayah, a tap on its marker (hit area 44pt), and the ayah-actions GlassButton in the reader's floating group. A tap on a word opens its WordChip meaning. VoiceOver and TalkBack get the same actions (play from here, tafsir, save, highlight, share) as accessibility actions on the ayah.
  • Never truncate, ellipsize or restyle the Quran text itself.

BottomSheet

Ayah actions in an inset glass sheet

An inset Liquid Glass sheet for contextual actions (ayah actions, reader settings, reciter choice) that leaves the Quran visible above and around it.

  • Provide: a title and meta line, a close action, and the sheet body (action grid, settings, list).
  • Classes: qa-sheet qa-glass, qa-sheet-grabber, qa-sheet-head, qa-action-grid with qa-action tiles.
  • Half height by default, inset space-2 from the screen edges with radius-xl corners, so the selected ayah stays in view.
  • Expanding to full height makes it opaque (glass-fallback) and adds the scrim behind it, for focus on longer content such as tafsir.
  • iOS 26+: use the system sheet (it adopts glass and the inset shape itself); do not add custom backgrounds to it. Android: a modal bottom sheet on glass-fallback.
  • The first action is the most likely one (play from here) and is the only brand tile.

Button

Primary, secondary, ghost and destructive

A pill-shaped text button for actions in the content layer: one primary per screen, secondary for alternatives, ghost for low-emphasis links and danger for destructive actions.

  • Provide: a short Arabic verb phrase as the label; optional leading icon.
  • Classes: qa-btn plus one of qa-btn-primary, qa-btn-secondary, qa-btn-ghost, qa-btn-danger.
  • Minimum height 44px, radius-pill, label in body weight 600.
  • Primary is brand with on-brand text. Never put two primary buttons on one screen.
  • Destructive buttons always say what they delete ("حذف التنزيلات"), never just "حذف".
  • Buttons that float over content (on the mushaf, in the player) are GlassButton, not this.

GlassButton

Floating icon buttons over the mushaf

A 44px circular icon button on Liquid Glass that floats over the reading surface, alone or grouped with related actions in one glass capsule.

  • Provide: an icon and an Arabic aria-label / accessibility label.
  • Classes: qa-icon-btn qa-glass for a single button; qa-glass-group qa-glass wrapping several qa-icon-btn for a group; qa-icon-btn-prominent for the one primary floating action (play), which is brand, not glass.
  • Group buttons that act on the same thing (reader settings, bookmark, ayah actions) into one capsule. Keep back navigation separate. The reader's title is plain text over the scroll edge effect, not a third capsule.
  • iOS 26+: GlassView with isInteractive so it responds to touch; groups in one GlassContainer. Fallback: glass-fallback with a line rim.
  • Never place a GlassButton on another glass surface, and never more than one group per screen edge.

HighlightSwatch

Pick a highlight colour for an ayah

A row of 44px colour buttons for highlighting an ayah, plus a "remove highlight" option.

  • Provide: the four highlight colours and the current one; each button has an Arabic name as its accessibility label.
  • Classes: qa-swatch buttons, each with a span filled with highlight-gold, highlight-green, highlight-blue or highlight-rose; the chosen one has aria-pressed="true", an ink ring and a check mark, so the choice never depends on colour.
  • Colours are told apart by lightness (light and sepia) and by name (every theme), not hue alone. Each swatch's accessibility label is its name («ذهبي»، «أخضر»، «أزرق»، «وردي»); in the notes list each highlight shows its colour name too.
  • Applying a colour updates the ayah behind the sheet immediately, with no confirm step.

MiniPlayer

Persistent glass mini-player above the tab bar

A Liquid Glass bar that shows the current recitation above the tab bar on every screen while audio is playing; tapping it opens the full player.

  • Provide: reciter name, surah and current ayah, progress (0–1), playing state, and handlers for play/pause and open.
  • Classes: qa-mini qa-glass, with qa-mini-progress (a 3px gold line along the top edge that grows from the leading edge, right in RTL), qa-mono monogram and a qa-icon-btn-prominent play/pause button.
  • The ayah in the caption updates with the audio timing, so readers can see where the recitation is without opening the player.
  • Place it space-3 above the tab bar; when the tab bar minimizes, the mini-player sits beside it in the same glass group. iOS 26+: it is the tab bar's bottom accessory (NativeTabs.BottomAccessory, inline placement when minimized). Expanded windows: the sidebar footer, solid, since the sidebar is the glass layer.
  • Swipe up to open the full player, swipe down on the full player to return. Never show both at once.

ReciterRow

Reciter list rows with style tag and play

A list row for a reciter: monogram, name, recitation style and what is playing or last played, with a play button.

  • Provide: reciter name, style (murattal or mujawwad, from the recitation data), an optional surah context, download state and a play handler.
  • Classes: qa-reciter with qa-mono (first letter of the family name in display type), qa-reciter-text, a qa-tag for the style and an icon button.
  • The row currently playing uses the prominent brand button; others use a secondary outlined button.
  • Use a monogram, not a photo, unless the content source supplies an approved reciter image.
  • In the Listen tab, long-press and the row's 44pt ··· button («خيارات القارئ») offer: preview 10 seconds, download all, set as default reciter. Onboarding rows only preview (play) and select, so they have no menu.

ScriptPicker

Choose the Quran font and script

A radio list in reader settings where the reader chooses how the Quran is written: Uthmani Hafs, Madani page-exact, IndoPak or colour-coded Tajweed.

  • Provide: the available scripts (name, a short description or a live sample rendered in that script's own font), the current choice, and download state for scripts not yet on the device.
  • Classes: fieldset with a legend, then one label.qa-option per script containing a radio input, qa-option-name and either qa-option-sample or a qa-caption.
  • Each sample renders in its own font family (quran, quran-indopak, …), never the interface font, so the reader sees exactly what they will get.
  • Selecting a script that is not downloaded starts its download and shows progress in the row; the reading view switches when it finishes.
  • Madani page-exact uses one font per page (604 pages), loaded lazily; say so in its description rather than showing a sample.
  • Tajweed shows a colour key in the reading view; the colours come with the Tajweed text data, not from this system's highlight tokens.

SegmentedControl

Display mode and reader theme

A glass pill of two to four mutually exclusive options, used for the display mode (Mushaf / Reading / Focus) and the reader appearance (Automatic / Light / Sepia / Dark).

  • Provide: a visible label, the options (short Arabic words) and the selected one; the change applies instantly with no save button.
  • Classes: qa-seg qa-glass with button children; the selected one carries aria-pressed="true" and sits on surface-raised with shadow-raised.
  • Use it inside the reader settings sheet or floating over content. On a plain surface background, the glass falls back to glass-fallback automatically when there is nothing behind it to refract.
  • Segments draw at 40px; with the 4px track padding each one's hit area is at least 44pt tall.
  • iOS 26+: the system segmented control adopts glass on its own; use it rather than a custom one.
  • More than four options (fonts, reciters, translations) go in a list such as ScriptPicker, not a segmented control.

SurahHeader

Surah cartouche and the page divider used in vertical scroll

The gold cartouche that opens each surah, plus the thin page divider that separates pages in the vertically scrolling reader.

  • Provide: the surah name (from the content source's surah metadata), and for the divider the page, juz and hizb numbers.
  • Classes: qa-surah-header containing the cartouche SVG and an h2.qa-surah-name in display-sm; qa-page-divider for the page break.
  • The cartouche is drawn in gold hairlines only; never fill it or add icons.
  • In Mushaf mode the cartouche sits where the mushaf layout places it. In Reading and Focus modes it opens the surah's first ayah.
  • The page divider replaces page swiping: every page break in the vertical scroll shows one, so readers still know which page they are on.

TabBar

Floating glass tab bar, full and minimized on scroll

The app's main navigation: a floating Liquid Glass capsule with four tabs (Home, Mushaf, Listen, My Quran) that minimizes to the current tab while the reader scrolls down.

  • Provide: four destinations, each with an icon (Home house, Mushaf book, Listen headphones, My Quran bookmark), an Arabic label and the current one marked aria-current="page".
  • Classes: qa-tabbar qa-glass with qa-tab items; minimized state qa-tabbar-min qa-glass showing only the current tab.
  • Floats space-4 from the screen sides and the home indicator; content scrolls underneath it.
  • Active tab: brand icon and label on a glass-selected pill. Inactive: ink-muted. Tab labels don't grow with Dynamic Type; long-press shows the large-content viewer instead.
  • Expanded windows replace the tab bar with the sidebar (same destinations, same glyphs). Selected item: brand icon and label on glass-selected. Hover and pressed: glass-selected at half strength, never the selected look. Keyboard focus: the 2px focus-ring. Each item is at least 48pt tall.
  • iOS 26+: use the system tab bar so it gets native glass and minimize-on-scroll (tabBarMinimizeBehavior .onScrollDown). Android: an opaque glass-fallback bar with a line top hairline.
  • In the full player and in Focus mode the tab bar hides completely.

WordChip

Word-by-word meaning; the recited word is active

A single Quran word with its meaning underneath, shown in the ayah actions sheet and when a reader taps a word.

  • Provide: the word text in the chosen script and its gloss from the word-by-word data in the reader's language.
  • Classes: qa-word with qa-word-ar (quran-word) and qa-word-gloss (label, in the gloss language's direction); qa-word-active marks the word being recited (highlight-active with a brand rim, matching the recited ayah).
  • Chips wrap in reading order, right to left, space-2 apart.
  • Tapping a chip plays that word's audio where word audio exists.
